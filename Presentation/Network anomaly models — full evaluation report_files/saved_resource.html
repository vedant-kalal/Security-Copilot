Verdict

The two models are genuinely complementary, not redundant — which is exactly the spec's "escalate if either flags it" design paying off.

  • DDoS: TranAD wins decisively. 0.88 F1 vs Isolation Forest's 0.26. A volumetric burst is obvious across a 60-second window and much less obvious flow-by-flow.
  • Wednesday's DoS floods (Hulk/GoldenEye/slowloris): Isolation Forest wins decisively. 0.70 F1 vs TranAD's 0.32. These attacks apparently have a distinctive per-flow signature (uniform packet timing/size) that gets diluted once averaged into a 60-second window alongside normal traffic.
  • PortScan, Bot, Infiltration: TranAD is the only one that works at all. Isolation Forest is at or near 0 F1 on all three; TranAD gets 0.56 / 0.11–0.42 / 0.48 respectively. Scanning and beaconing are inherently temporal patterns — a single flow's stats don't show it, a window of connection activity does.
  • Brute force remains an open gap for both. Isolation Forest tops out at F1 0.12 best-case; TranAD's AUC is actually below 0.5 (0.35) — worse than random. Neither per-flow stats nor 60s window aggregates seem to carry the FTP/SSH-Patator signature. This is the one area that still needs real work — likely a feature/window design specific to login-attempt cadence, not a tuning fix.

Recommendation: keep both models running in production as designed — together they cover five of six attack types well. Brute-force detection is the one clear next investment, and it probably needs a purpose-built signal (e.g. failed-auth rate over a shorter window) rather than more of what's already been tried on these two models.
